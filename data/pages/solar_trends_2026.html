01 · Market overview

Solar’s next constraint is not supply

Solar entered 2026 with a paradox: the equipment required to produce clean electricity has rarely been more available, yet the systems needed to absorb that electricity are increasingly scarce. Years of manufacturing investment have created deep module supply and lower hardware prices. Interconnection queues, flexible demand and network capacity now set the pace in many mature markets.

GreenScope’s central case places annual photovoltaic additions at 612 GW, extending a decade of rapid expansion without repeating the extreme price declines of the previous two years. Utility-scale projects still deliver most new output, but commercial rooftops and community solar are broadening participation in markets where land, permitting or transmission is constrained.

02 · Global deployment

Growth is global, but far from uniform

Asia-Pacific remains the volume engine, supported by large procurement programmes, dense manufacturing networks and rising commercial electricity demand. Europe’s pace moderates as grid queues lengthen, while North America’s growth is shaped by domestic-content rules and delayed transmission projects. New procurement in the Middle East, Africa and Latin America adds a smaller but increasingly consequential layer of demand.

03 · Technology & manufacturing

Efficiency gains move beyond the cell

Mainstream module efficiency continues to improve incrementally, but project-level engineering now creates as much value as headline cell performance. Higher-voltage architectures, smarter inverters and refined tracking algorithms reduce balance-of-system costs and improve output during shoulder hours.

Manufacturing capacity remains concentrated, even as governments support local plants. New facilities are most competitive when they combine reliable low-cost power, automated production and access to upstream materials. Smaller factories built only to satisfy policy incentives face difficult economics when global utilisation rates are low.

“The cheapest panel does not necessarily produce the lowest-cost system. Interconnection timing, curtailment risk and dispatchability now decide which projects create durable value.”

04 · Solar + storage

Batteries reshape the solar production curve

Co-located storage has shifted from an optional enhancement to a core design choice in high-penetration markets. In GreenScope’s project sample, 38% of utility solar capacity reaching final investment decision includes at least two hours of battery storage. Four-hour systems are common where evening capacity has a clear market value.

  • Shared infrastructure: hybrid projects use one interconnection point and can make better use of export capacity.
  • Price shaping: batteries move a portion of midday generation into the evening peak, reducing exposure to low or negative prices.
  • Firm delivery: contracted projects increasingly offer shaped output rather than undifferentiated energy.
05 · Grid challenges

Integration becomes an operating discipline

High solar penetration changes the daily rhythm of power systems. Midday prices soften, ramping requirements increase toward sunset and maintenance planning must account for more volatile net demand. These effects are manageable, but only when network planning, market rules and flexible resources evolve together.

Near-term progress will come from faster interconnection studies, dynamic export limits, targeted network upgrades and tariffs that reward flexible consumption. Data centres, industrial heat and managed EV charging can become valuable sources of demand when their operations respond to periods of plentiful renewable output.

06 · 2026 outlook

From deployment race to system value

Solar’s cost and construction advantages remain intact. The next phase rewards developers that understand the grid as well as the module. Markets able to coordinate storage, transmission and flexible demand will capture more of solar’s declining cost curve; those that treat integration as an afterthought will experience rising curtailment and weaker project returns.

GreenScope expects installation volumes to remain elevated through 2027, with the composition of growth gradually moving toward hybrid projects, commercial rooftops and markets with strong daytime demand. The defining metric will no longer be capacity alone, but how effectively each new megawatt serves the wider energy system.
